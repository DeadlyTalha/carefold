# Claim Denial Code Interpreter & Action Guide

Health insurance claim denials and payment adjustments are communicated via Claim Adjustment Reason Codes (CARC) and Remittance Advice Remark Codes (RARC). Understanding these alphanumeric codes allows patients and billing advocates to pinpoint the exact root cause of a denied medical claim and take appropriate corrective action.

## 1. Top CARC Codes & Remediation Protocols

### CARC 50: Non-Covered Service
- **Definition**: "These are non-covered services because this is not deemed a 'medical necessity' by the payer."
- **Root Cause**: The insurer determined that the treatment, test, or hospital day was experimental, investigational, or did not meet specific clinical coverage criteria.
- **Action Strategy**: File a formal Level 1 Medical Necessity Appeal. Submit clinical records, relevant national clinical guidelines (e.g., NCCN, AHA, ACR), and a signed Letter of Medical Necessity from the attending physician.

### CARC 197: Precertification / Prior Authorization Absent
- **Definition**: "Precertification/authorization/notification/pre-treatment absence."
- **Root Cause**: The provider failed to obtain an approved prior authorization number before the service was rendered, or the procedure code billed did not match the authorized code.
- **Action Strategy**:
  - Request that the provider submit a retroactive authorization request if permitted under plan rules.
  - If the service was an emergency or urgent intervention, invoke federal EMTALA protections and plan provisions that prohibit prior authorization for emergency evaluations.

### CARC 96: Non-Covered Charges
- **Definition**: "Non-covered charges. At least one Remark Code must be provided."
- **Root Cause**: The billed benefit is explicitly excluded under the policy contract (e.g., cosmetic procedures, specific nutritional therapies, experimental off-label uses).
- **Action Strategy**: Inspect the Summary Plan Description (SPD) for exact exclusion language. Determine if an exception applies or if the service qualifies under secondary diagnostic indications.

### CARC 45: Exceeds Maximum Allowable Charge
- **Definition**: "Charge exceeds fee schedule/maximum allowable or contracted/legislated fee arrangement."
- **Root Cause**: Contractual adjustment between an in-network provider and the insurer.
- **Action Strategy**: This is generally a contractual write-off. The patient is NOT responsible for paying this balance if the provider is in-network. Verify that the provider has not balance-billed the patient.

## 2. No Surprises Act Protections (Out-of-Network Emergency & Ancillary Care)
Under the federal No Surprises Act:
- Out-of-network emergency room facility and physician charges must be processed at in-network cost-sharing levels.
- Out-of-network ancillary providers (anesthesiologists, radiologists, pathologists, assistant surgeons) at in-network hospitals cannot balance-bill patients beyond in-network cost-sharing amounts without voluntary prior consent.

## 3. Immediate Action Checklist Upon Receiving a Denied Claim
- [ ] Cross-reference the Explanation of Benefits (EOB) with the actual bill from the provider.
- [ ] Confirm whether the patient responsibility line is listed as deductible, copay, coinsurance, or non-covered.
- [ ] Note the date of the formal denial letter to establish the 180-day appeal deadline.

## 4. Emergency Care Notice
Insurance claim issues are strictly administrative and financial. For any acute physical emergency or life-threatening crisis, call 911 immediately.
