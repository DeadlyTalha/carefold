# Vertigo & Dizziness Episode Log

Dizziness is a broad clinical complaint that encompasses true rotational vertigo (a false sensation that you or your surroundings are spinning), unsteadiness or disequilibrium (loss of balance without spinning), lightheadedness/presyncope (feeling faint or near syncope), and oscillopsia (visual bouncing when walking). This log helps patients differentiate and record dizziness spells for vestibular otolaryngology evaluations.

## 1. Differentiating Dizziness Types
- **True Vertigo**: Sensation of the room spinning or tumbling. Suggests an inner ear vestibular disorder (e.g., BPPV, vestibular neuritis, Ménière's disease) or central vestibular pathway involvement.
- **Disequilibrium / Imbalance**: Unsteadiness in the feet while walking or standing, with no spinning sensation while seated. Common in peripheral neuropathy, bilateral vestibular loss, or musculoskeletal deficits.
- **Presyncope**: Lightheadedness, graying vision, or feeling about to pass out, often occurring upon standing up quickly (orthostatic hypotension or cardiovascular causes).

## 3. Important Clinical Clues
- **Brief Seconds (<1 Minute)**: Often points toward Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV) triggered by calcium carbonate crystals (otoconia) displaced into semicircular canals.
- **Hours (20 min to 12 hours)**: Accompanied by unilateral ear fullness, fluctuating low-frequency hearing loss, and tinnitus, often points toward endolymphatic hydrops / Ménière's disease.
- **Days (Constant)**: Severe continuous spinning lasting 24–72 hours with nausea points toward acute vestibular neuritis or labyrinthitis.

## 4. Consultation Questions for Your ENT / Neurotologist
1. "Does my history suggest a peripheral inner ear issue like BPPV, Ménière's disease, or vestibular neuritis?"
2. "Would an in-office Dix-Hallpike positional test and Epley canalith repositioning maneuver be helpful today?"
3. "Would videonystagmography (VNG), rotary chair testing, or vestibular evoked myogenic potentials (VEMP) clarify my balance function?"
4. "Would vestibular physical therapy rehabilitation (VRT) help reduce my unsteadiness and improve gaze stabilization?"

## 5. Emergency Warning Signs (Call 911 / Emergency Room)
Never assume dizziness is simply an inner ear issue if accompanied by "red flag" neurological signs. Contact 911 immediately if vertigo or dizziness is accompanied by:
- Sudden weakness, numbness, or paralysis of the face, arm, or leg (especially on one side).
- Slurred speech or difficulty understanding words.
- Sudden double vision or inability to swallow.
- Severe sudden headache ("thunderclap" headache).
- Complete inability to stand or sit upright without falling (acute ataxia).
