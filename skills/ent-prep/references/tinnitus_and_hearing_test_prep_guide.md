# Tinnitus Diary & Audiometry Preparation Guide

Tinnitus is the perception of sound in the absence of an external acoustic stimulus. Often described as ringing, buzzing, hissing, or pulsing, tinnitus frequently accompanies sensorineural hearing loss. This guide prepares patients for comprehensive audiologic evaluations and helps document tinnitus severity and daily functional impact.

## 1. Tinnitus Characterization Worksheet
- **Acoustic Characteristics**:
  - Sound Quality: High-pitched ringing, low humming, ocean roaring, clicking, or pulsatile whooshing (in sync with heartbeat).
  - Laterality: Right ear only, left ear only, bilateral, or generalized inside head.
  - Constant vs. Intermittent: Present 100% of waking hours, or fluctuating with quiet environments, stress, or jaw clenching.
- **Pulsatile Tinnitus Warning**: If tinnitus sounds like a rhythmic heartbeat or rushing water in sync with your pulse, explicitly notify your doctor, as this may require vascular imaging.

## 2. Tinnitus Handicap Inventory (THI) Functional Self-Assessment
Check how tinnitus impacts your daily life:
- [ ] Does your tinnitus make it difficult for you to fall asleep at night?
- [ ] Does your tinnitus interfere with your ability to concentrate on work, reading, or conversations?
- [ ] Do you feel irritable, frustrated, or anxious because of your tinnitus?
- [ ] Does tinnitus make it difficult to hear conversations in restaurants or group settings?

## 3. Preparing for a Comprehensive Hearing Evaluation (Audiogram)
A standard diagnostic audiometric battery includes several non-invasive tests:
- **Pure-Tone Audiometry**: Measures the softest sounds you can hear across frequencies from 250 Hz to 8000 Hz via air conduction (headphones) and bone conduction (oscillator on mastoid bone).
- **Speech Reception Threshold (SRT) & Word Recognition Score (WRS)**: Evaluates speech clarity and how accurately you can repeat single-syllable words presented at comfortable listening volumes.
- **Tympanometry & Acoustic Reflexes**: Evaluates middle ear pressure, eardrum mobility, and ossicular chain function.
- **Otoacoustic Emissions (OAEs)**: Assesses outer hair cell function in the cochlea.

## 4. Consultation Questions for Your Otolaryngologist / Audiologist
1. "Does my audiogram show symmetric or asymmetric hearing loss, and is the deficit sensorineural, conductive, or mixed?"
2. "If my hearing loss is asymmetric or accompanied by unilateral tinnitus, is an MRI of the internal auditory canals indicated to rule out a retrocochlear lesion (such as an acoustic neuroma/vestibular schwannoma)?"
3. "Would modern digital hearing aids with integrated sound therapy or white-noise maskers reduce the contrast of my tinnitus?"

## 5. Acute Emergency Protocol
Sudden sensorineural hearing loss (SSNHL)—defined as a rapid unexplained drop in hearing in one ear occurring over 72 hours or less—is a clinical emergency. Seek immediate emergency medical care (call 911 or visit an emergency department) within 24–48 hours for urgent evaluation and potential corticosteroid therapy.
