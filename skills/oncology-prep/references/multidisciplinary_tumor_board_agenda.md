# Multidisciplinary Tumor Board Agenda & Consultation Guide

A multidisciplinary tumor board (MDTB) brings together medical oncologists, surgical oncologists, radiation oncologists, radiologists, and pathologists to review complex cancer cases and establish a consensus treatment strategy. This guide helps patients and caregivers understand and prepare for consultations following a tumor board review.

## 1. Structure of the Multidisciplinary Oncology Team
- **Medical Oncologist**: Oversees systemic therapies (chemotherapy, immunotherapy, hormonal therapy, targeted therapies).
- **Surgical Oncologist**: Evaluates resectability, surgical margins, lymph node dissection, and timing of surgical interventions.
- **Radiation Oncologist**: Assesses radiation modalities (external beam, IMRT, SBRT, brachytherapy) for curative or palliative intent.
- **Pathologist**: Analyzes tissue biopsies, histological tumor grading, margin status, and molecular biomarkers (e.g., HER2, ER/PR, EGFR, KRAS, PD-L1, MMR/MSI).
- **Diagnostic Radiologist**: Reviews CT, MRI, PET-CT, and ultrasound imaging to stage disease extent and assess treatment response.

## 2. Pre-Consultation Question Framework
Use these structured categories to organize inquiries for your treating physician:

### A. Consensus & Sequence of Treatment
1. "What was the consensus recommendation of the tumor board regarding the sequence of my therapy (e.g., neoadjuvant chemotherapy before surgery vs. upfront surgery)?"
2. "Were there differing clinical opinions among the specialists, and what factors determined the final consensus?"
3. "What are the primary goals of this proposed sequence (curative intent vs. disease stabilization vs. symptom palliation)?"

### B. Pathology & Molecular Biomarkers
1. "What specific molecular mutations, receptor expressions, or genomic markers were identified in my tumor biopsy?"
2. "Do my biomarker findings qualify me for targeted oral therapies or immune checkpoint inhibitors?"
3. "Is there sufficient tissue remaining if additional genomic sequencing (next-generation sequencing / NGS) is recommended in the future?"

### C. Imaging & Restaging Milestones
1. "When will follow-up imaging (CT/PET-CT/MRI) be scheduled to evaluate tumor response to the current phase?"
2. "What specific criteria (e.g., RECIST response, shrinkage, metabolic activity decline) will determine whether the current treatment is successful?"

## 3. Coordinating Supportive Care Services
A comprehensive cancer treatment plan incorporates supportive care:
- **Oncology Nutrition**: Guidance for unintentional weight loss, cachexia, or swallowing difficulties.
- **Physical & Occupational Therapy**: Pre-habilitation and lymphedema prevention strategies.
- **Social Work & Financial Navigation**: Assistance with insurance pre-authorizations, medical leave, and transportation.
- **Psychosocial Oncology / Support Groups**: Resources for coping with emotional distress and family adjustment.

## 4. Emergency Red Flags
Consultations must never delay acute medical care. Contact emergency services (911) immediately if the patient develops severe difficulty breathing, sudden confusion, uncontrolled pain crises, severe bleeding, or acute signs of infection/fever during active therapy.
