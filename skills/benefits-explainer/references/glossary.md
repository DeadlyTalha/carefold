# Health Insurance Glossary

Plain-language definitions of core health insurance terms.

### 1. Premium
The fixed amount you (and/or your employer) pay each month to maintain active health insurance coverage. Paying your premium keeps your policy active, but premium payments do not count toward your deductible or out-of-pocket maximum.

### 2. Deductible
The predetermined amount you must pay out-of-pocket for covered medical services before your insurance plan begins sharing costs. For example, with a $1,500 deductible, you pay 100% of negotiated rates for non-preventive care until your cumulative spending reaches $1,500 for that plan year.

### 3. Copayment (Copay)
A fixed dollar amount you pay for a specific covered healthcare service at the time of care (e.g., $25 for a primary care doctor visit, $50 for a specialist, or $15 for generic tier-1 medications). Copays often apply even before your annual deductible is satisfied.

### 4. Coinsurance
Your percentage share of the costs of a covered healthcare service after your deductible has been met. For instance, if your plan has an 80/20 coinsurance structure, your insurance pays 80% of the allowed amount, and you are responsible for the remaining 20%.

### 5. Out-of-Pocket Maximum (OOPM)
The absolute legal maximum amount you are required to pay for covered in-network healthcare services during a policy year. Once your combined spending on deductibles, copayments, and coinsurance reaches this threshold, the health plan pays 100% of all covered in-network medical expenses for the rest of the plan year. Premiums and out-of-network balance billing do not count toward this limit.

### 6. In-Network vs. Out-of-Network
- **In-Network**: Healthcare providers, clinics, and hospitals that have signed contracts with your insurance company to provide services at pre-negotiated discounted rates.
- **Out-of-Network**: Providers who have not contracted with your plan. Out-of-network services typically have much higher deductibles, higher coinsurance rates, or may not be covered at all, and you may be subject to balance billing where permitted.

### 7. Prior Authorization (Pre-Certification)
A mandatory requirement by the health insurer requiring your clinician to submit clinical documentation demonstrating medical necessity before a procedure, advanced imaging scan, or specialty medication will be approved for coverage. Receiving prior authorization does not guarantee 100% payment, but performing services without it typically results in a complete claim denial.

### 8. Explanation of Benefits (EOB)
A financial statement sent by your insurance provider detailing how a healthcare claim was processed. An EOB is **not a bill**. It displays:
- The amount billed by the healthcare provider.
- The insurer's allowable negotiated discount.
- The amount paid by the insurance plan.
- The remaining balance that the healthcare provider may bill you directly.

### 9. Formulary & Prescription Drug Tiers
The approved list of prescription medications covered by your insurance plan, organized into pricing tiers:
- **Tier 1**: Low-cost generic medications (lowest copay).
- **Tier 2**: Preferred brand-name medications and higher-cost generics.
- **Tier 3**: Non-preferred brand-name medications.
- **Tier 4 / Specialty**: High-cost biologics and specialty therapies (often subject to coinsurance rather than a flat copay).

### 10. Summary of Benefits and Coverage (SBC)
A standardized, federally mandated summary document that allows consumers to compare health plan costs, coverage details, exceptions, and cost-sharing examples in a uniform format.
