# Renal Laboratory Interpretation & Discussion Guide

A structured guide to understanding kidney function markers and formulating meaningful questions for your nephrologist.

## 1. Key Renal Laboratory Markers

| Test Name | Typical Purpose | What It Reflects | Common Questions for Clinician |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Serum Creatinine** | Waste product of muscle metabolism | Filtered primarily by kidneys; rising values indicate decreased clearance | "How does my current creatinine compare to my baseline from last year?" |
| **eGFR (Estimated GFR)** | Mathematical estimate of filtration rate | Approximate percentage of normal kidney filtration capacity | "Is my eGFR stable, fluctuating, or showing a persistent downward trend?" |
| **BUN (Blood Urea Nitrogen)** | Byproduct of dietary protein breakdown | Clearance by kidneys; influenced by hydration, protein intake, and GI bleeding | "Does my BUN elevation reflect dehydration or a change in kidney function?" |
| **UACR (Urine Albumin-Creatinine Ratio)** | Measures protein leakage into urine | Glomerular barrier integrity; early marker of vascular or kidney damage | "What is my current UACR, and are my blood pressure medications protecting my kidneys?" |
| **Serum Potassium (K+)** | Vital electrolyte for nerve and muscle function | Kidneys regulate excretion; both high and low levels carry cardiac risks | "Is my potassium within a safe range, and do I need to restrict high-potassium foods?" |
| **Serum Phosphorus & Calcium** | Mineral and bone disorder balance | Managed by kidneys and parathyroid hormone (PTH) | "Do I need to monitor phosphorus in my diet or take any phosphate binders?" |
| **Serum Bicarbonate (CO2)** | Acid-base balance regulation | Declining levels indicate metabolic acidosis in advancing CKD | "Is my bicarbonate level normal, or should we discuss oral sodium bicarbonate therapy?" |

## 2. Important Principles for Reviewing Labs
- **Longitudinal Trajectory Over Single Numbers**: A single creatinine spike can be caused by dehydration, vigorous exercise, or acute illness. Nephrologists assess trends over 3 to 12 months.
- **Medication Renal Clearance**: Many prescription and over-the-counter drugs are cleared by the kidneys. Always inform your doctor of every medication.
- **Nephrotoxic Exposure Warning**: Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs like ibuprofen, naproxen) and iodinated contrast dyes can cause acute kidney injury.

## 3. High-Value Questions for Your Nephrologist
- What is the underlying cause of my kidney function decline (e.g., hypertension, diabetes, glomerulonephritis)?
- What is my target blood pressure for optimal kidney protection?
- Should I consult with a specialized renal dietitian regarding my daily protein, sodium, and potassium intake?
- Are there kidney-protective medications (such as SGLT2 inhibitors or ACE inhibitors/ARBs) appropriate for my stage?
