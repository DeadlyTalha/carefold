# Copay Assistance & Patient Foundation Directory

Prescription medication affordability programs provide billions of dollars in annual financial relief to insured, underinsured, and uninsured patients. Understanding which assistance pathways match your insurance status is critical, as federal anti-kickback laws establish strict distinctions between commercial and government-sponsored health coverage.

## 1. Categorization by Insurance Coverage Status

### Category A: Commercially Insured Patients (Employer / ACA Marketplace Plans)
- **Manufacturer Copay Savings Cards / Coupons**:
  - Provided directly by pharmaceutical manufacturers to offset commercial copays and coinsurance for brand-name and specialty drugs.
  - Can often reduce patient out-of-pocket costs to \$0–\$35 per month up to an annual maximum benefit (e.g., \$5,000–\$15,000/year).
  - **Federal Restriction**: By federal law, manufacturer copay cards **cannot be used** by individuals enrolled in Medicare, Medicaid, TRICARE, or VA health programs.
- **Copay Accumulator Adjustment Programs**:
  - Be aware that some insurers utilize "accumulator adjusters" that prevent manufacturer copay card payments from counting toward your annual deductible or out-of-pocket maximum. Check plan documents for "copay maximizer" or "accumulator" clauses.

### Category B: Medicare Enrollees (Part D / Medicare Advantage)
- **Extra Help (Low-Income Subsidy / LIS)**:
  - Federal program that assists Medicare beneficiaries with limited income and resources in paying Part D premiums, deductibles, and prescription copays (limiting copays to ~\$4.50 for generics and ~\$11.20 for brand drugs).
- **Independent 501(c)(3) Charitable Copay Foundations**:
  - Because Medicare beneficiaries cannot use manufacturer copay cards, non-profit foundations provide disease-specific financial grants.
  - Examples of leading foundations:
    - **Patient Access Network (PAN) Foundation**: Grants for oncology, autoimmune, chronic conditions.
    - **HealthWell Foundation**: Grants for copays, premiums, and pediatric chronic diseases.
    - **Patient Advocate Foundation (PAF) Co-Pay Relief**: Assistance for targeted chronic and life-threatening illnesses.
    - **CancerCare Co-Payment Assistance Foundation**: Dedicated assistance for cancer treatments.

### Category C: Uninsured Patients
- **Manufacturer Patient Assistance Programs (PAPs)**:
  - Free medication provided directly to uninsured patients meeting income thresholds (typically 200%–500% of the Federal Poverty Level).
  - Requires an application signed by both patient and prescribing physician.
- **Generic Cash Discount Pharmacies**:
  - **Mark Cuban Cost Plus Drug Company**: Sells hundreds of generic medications at manufacturer wholesale cost + 15% markup + \$5 pharmacy fee.
  - **GoodRx / SingleCare / Retail Cash Lists**: Discount coupons for retail pharmacy pick-up.

## 2. Application Preparation Checklist
- [ ] Proof of Household Income (Most recent IRS Form 1040, W-2, or Social Security SSA-1099 statement).
- [ ] Front and back copy of Health Insurance Card / Medicare Card.
- [ ] Prescribing Physician Information (NPI, clinic address, clinic contact).
- [ ] Signed Patient Authorization and HIPAA Disclosure Form.

## 3. Urgent Health Crisis Notice
Financial assistance programs are administrative resources. In any medical emergency, call 911 immediately or proceed to the nearest emergency department.
