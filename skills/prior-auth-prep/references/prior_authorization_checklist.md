# Prior Authorization Checklist & Clinical Submission Guide

Prior authorization (PA) is a utilization management tool used by health insurance plans to determine whether a prescribed medication, diagnostic test, or clinical procedure will be covered under plan benefits. This checklist helps patients, caregivers, and medical office staff assemble complete submission packets to prevent unnecessary delays and denials.

## 1. Patient & Policy Verification
- **Patient Full Name & DOB**: [Verify matches insurance card exactly]
- **Subscriber / Member ID Number**: [Including alphanumeric prefix and suffix]
- **Group Number & Plan Type**: [e.g., Commercial HMO/PPO, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid MCO]
- **Pharmacy Benefit Manager (PBM)**: [e.g., CVS Caremark, Express Scripts, OptumRx]
- **Prescriber Information**: NPI number, DEA number, state license, clinic address, and direct prior authorization contact phone/fax.

## 2. Clinical Documentation Requirements
Ensure the following clinical records are attached to the initial prior authorization submission:
- [ ] **Exact Diagnosis & ICD-10 Code**: Clearly stated in recent clinical encounter notes.
- [ ] **Chart Notes from Last 6 Months**: Documenting clinical rationale, disease severity, functional impairment, and lack of improvement on conservative management.
- [ ] **Objective Diagnostic Evidence**: Relevant laboratory results, imaging reports (CT, MRI, ultrasound), biopsy pathology, or endoscopic findings.
- [ ] **Comprehensive Medication History**: Documentation of all previously tried drugs for this condition, including exact start/stop dates, highest tolerated dosage, reason for discontinuation (inefficacy, intolerable side effects, or drug-drug contraindications).
- [ ] **Specific Drug / Procedure Details**: Exact NDC (National Drug Code), HCPCS code, dosage, quantity, anticipated duration of therapy, and administration route (in-office infusion vs. self-administered specialty pharmacy).

## 3. Insurer Response Timeline Milestones
Under standard insurance regulations:
- **Standard (Non-Urgent) Review**: Typically resolved within 72 hours to 15 business days depending on state regulations and plan type.
- **Expedited (Urgent) Review**: Typically required within 24 to 72 hours when standard timelines could seriously jeopardize the patient's life, health, or ability to regain maximum function.

## 4. Next Steps Upon Initial Approval or Denial
- **If Approved**: Obtain the PA Reference Number, approved effective dates (e.g., valid for 6 or 12 months), and quantity limits. Confirm the prescription has been routed to an in-network specialty pharmacy.
- **If Denied**: Request the formal written denial letter citing the specific medical necessity criteria not met, deadline for filing an appeal, and instructions for scheduling a peer-to-peer review.

## 5. Emergency Warning
Prior authorization workflows are administrative. If you or a family member experience an acute medical emergency, call 911 immediately or visit the nearest emergency room. Emergency treatment does not require prior authorization under federal EMTALA regulations.
