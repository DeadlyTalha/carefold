# Morning Stiffness & Fatigue Timing Protocol

The duration of morning joint stiffness (the "gel phenomenon") is a primary clinical metric used by rheumatologists to distinguish inflammatory arthritis from mechanical joint degeneration (osteoarthritis). This protocol guides patients in measuring, timing, and recording morning stiffness and fatigue levels.

## 1. Understanding Inflammatory vs. Mechanical Stiffness
- **Inflammatory Stiffness (e.g., Rheumatoid Arthritis, Lupus)**: Often lasts longer than 30–60 minutes after waking. Movement and warm showers gradually alleviate stiffness.
- **Mechanical Stiffness (e.g., Osteoarthritis)**: Typically lasts less than 15–30 minutes upon waking and may worsen later in the day with excessive joint load.

## 2. Measurement Protocol
Follow these guidelines each morning for 7–14 consecutive days prior to your clinic visit:
1. **Time of Waking**: Note the exact time you wake up and first attempt to move your joints.
2. **First Peak Stiffness Rating**: On a scale of 0 (completely loose) to 10 (completely rigid), record your initial stiffness score upon waking.
3. **Recovery Milestone**: Note the exact time at which your stiffness reaches maximum improvement or plateaus for the day.
4. **Calculated Duration**: Total minutes elapsed from wake time to maximum improvement time.

## 3. Systemic Fatigue Assessment
Autoimmune fatigue is distinct from ordinary tiredness and represents systemic cytokine activation.
- **Fatigue Timing**: Does fatigue peak in mid-afternoon or is it present immediately upon waking?
- **Brain Fog**: Note difficulties with focus, word recall, or processing speed during high-fatigue days.
- **Physical Exertion Tolerance**: How long can you engage in light housework or walking before needing recumbent rest?

## 4. Consultation Question Bank
1. "My morning stiffness consistently exceeds 60 minutes. Does this indicate that my rheumatoid disease activity score (CDAI/DAS28) is insufficiently controlled?"
2. "Could my persistent fatigue be related to autoimmune inflammation, anemia of chronic disease, or thyroid involvement?"
3. "Would assistive devices, occupational therapy, or warm water physical therapy help maintain my morning mobility?"

## 5. Emergency Protocol
Never delay emergency care for sudden severe symptoms. Contact emergency medical services (911) immediately if you experience acute fever with an inflamed joint, sudden inability to walk, or acute chest pain.
