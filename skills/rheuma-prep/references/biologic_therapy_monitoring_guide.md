# Biologic Therapy Safety & Monitoring Guide

Biologic medications and targeted synthetic disease-modifying agents (tsDMARDs) target specific components of the immune system to halt joint destruction and autoimmune inflammation. Because these agents modulate immune function, patients require rigorous pre-treatment screening and ongoing safety surveillance.

## 1. Classes of Targeted Therapies
- **TNF-alpha Inhibitors**: (e.g., Adalimumab, Etanercept, Infliximab, Golimumab, Certolizumab).
- **IL-6 Receptor Antagonists**: (e.g., Tocilizumab, Sarilumab).
- **T-Cell Costimulation Modulators**: (e.g., Abatacept).
- **B-Cell Depleting Agents**: (e.g., Rituximab).
- **JAK Inhibitors**: (e.g., Tofacitinib, Baricitinib, Upadacitinib).

## 2. Pre-Treatment Baseline Screening Checklist
Before initiating any biologic therapy, ensure these mandatory baseline screenings have been completed:
- [ ] **Tuberculosis (TB) Screening**: Interferon-gamma release assay (QuantiFERON-TB Gold or T-SPOT) or tuberculin skin test (PPD), plus chest radiograph if indicated.
- [ ] **Viral Hepatitis Panel**: Hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg), core antibody (anti-HBc), and Hepatitis C antibody.
- [ ] **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**: Baseline absolute neutrophil count (ANC) and platelets.
- [ ] **Hepatic & Renal Panels**: Baseline ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase, and serum creatinine.
- [ ] **Immunization Review**: Verification of non-live vaccines (Pneumococcal, Shingrix, COVID-19, Influenza). Note: Live attenuated vaccines are generally contraindicated while on biologic therapy.

## 3. Routine Safety Surveillance & Injection Reactions
- **Routine Laboratory Cadence**: Follow your rheumatologist's schedule for quarterly or biannual blood draws (CBC, LFTs, lipid panels for JAK inhibitors).
- **Subcutaneous Injection Site Reactions**: Document erythema, mild swelling, or itching at the injection site. Note whether symptoms resolve within 48–72 hours.
- **Infection Monitoring**: Report any localized infections, persistent cough, burning with urination, or skin boils to your doctor before administering your next dose.

## 4. Consultation Discussion Topics
Bring these questions to your regular rheumatology review:
1. "What is our scheduled schedule for surveillance blood work while I am on this biologic?"
2. "If I develop symptoms of a cold, sore throat, or dental infection, should I hold my upcoming biologic dose?"
3. "Are there any upcoming surgical or dental procedures that require temporary discontinuation of my therapy?"

## 5. Emergency Warning Symptoms
Patients taking biologics or immunosuppressive agents face increased risk of serious infections. Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department immediately if you develop:
- High fever (>101°F) with shaking chills.
- Confusion, severe stiff neck with fever, or sudden shortness of breath.
- Rapidly spreading, painful skin infection or abscess.
- Anaphylactic symptoms (facial swelling, throat tightness, hives, wheezing) following an injection or infusion.
