# Podiatry Red Flag Warning Protocol

Clinical decision thresholds to distinguish routine podiatry clinic discussion topics from life-threatening emergencies.

## Immediate 911 / Emergency Room Red Flags
Stop using this application and call 911 immediately if you experience:
1. **Acute Limb Ischemia**: Sudden severe foot pain, a foot that feels cold or looks pale or mottled, or sudden loss of movement or sensation in the foot.
2. **Spreading Soft-Tissue Infection**: Redness, warmth, or swelling rapidly spreading up the leg, especially with fever, chills, or feeling very unwell (potential sign of cellulitis or sepsis).
3. **Black or Dead Tissue**: Any area of black, leathery, or foul-smelling tissue on the foot or toes (potential sign of gangrene).
4. **Uncontrolled Bleeding**: Bleeding from a foot wound that will not stop after 10 minutes of firm pressure, especially while on blood thinners.
5. **Severe Acute Dyspnea or Chest Pain**: Shortness of breath or chest pain alongside a swollen, painful leg (potential sign of pulmonary embolism from a DVT).
6. **Stroke Signs (FAST)**: Sudden facial drooping, unilateral arm weakness, slurred speech, or acute loss of vision.

## Urgent Clinic Contact Signs (Call Physician Within 24 Hours)
Contact your podiatrist's office or on-call nurse if you notice:
- A new wound, blister, cut, or ingrown nail on the foot that is not healing or is becoming more painful.
- Increasing redness, warmth, or swelling around a known wound or callus.
- New or worsening numbness, tingling, or burning in the feet, especially at night.
- A sudden change in walking pattern, balance, or the ability to bear weight.
- Discoloration of the toes (blue, purple, or dusky) even without pain.
- Fever, chills, or feeling generally unwell alongside any foot symptom.
