# Thyroid & Metabolic Health Question Bank

A structured reference guide for organizing thyroid symptoms, laboratory tests, and consultation topics.

## 1. Understanding Common Thyroid Laboratory Tests

| Test Name | What It Measures | Clinical Context | Discussion Questions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **TSH (Thyroid Stimulating Hormone)** | Pituitary hormone signaling thyroid | High TSH usually indicates underactive thyroid (hypo); low TSH indicates overactive (hyper) | "Is my current TSH at my personal optimal target, or just within the broad lab range?" |
| **Free T4 (Thyroxine)** | Active circulating unbound storage hormone | Converted in body tissues into active T3; reflects direct hormone output | "How does my Free T4 correlate with my energy levels and symptoms?" |
| **Free T3 (Triiodothyronine)** | Active circulating metabolic hormone | Responsible for direct metabolic action on tissues | "Would checking my Free T3 provide helpful insights into my symptoms?" |
| **TPO Antibodies (Thyroperoxidase)** | Autoimmune antibodies targeting thyroid | Elevated in Hashimoto's thyroiditis; confirms autoimmune etiology | "Do my antibody levels confirm an autoimmune thyroid condition?" |

## 2. Thyroid Symptom Chronology Checklist
Record symptoms experienced over the past 4–8 weeks to present to your endocrinologist:

### Symptoms Often Associated with Hypothyroidism (Underactive)
- [ ] Persistent fatigue despite adequate sleep
- [ ] Increased sensitivity to cold temperatures (hands/feet cold, requiring extra layers)
- [ ] Dry skin, brittle nails, or diffuse hair thinning
- [ ] Unexplained modest weight gain or difficulty losing weight
- [ ] Constipation or sluggish digestion
- [ ] Brain fog, memory lapses, or slowed cognition
- [ ] Muscle aches, joint stiffness, or cramps

### Symptoms Often Associated with Hyperthyroidism (Overactive)
- [ ] Heat intolerance and profuse sweating
- [ ] Palpitations, rapid resting heart rate, or pounding pulse
- [ ] Unintentional weight loss despite increased appetite
- [ ] Fine hand tremors or muscle weakness
- [ ] Nervousness, anxiety, irritability, or insomnia
- [ ] Frequent bowel movements

## 3. Medication Administration Review
Thyroid hormone replacement (e.g., levothyroxine) requires precise administration:
- [ ] Taken first thing in the morning with a full glass of water.
- [ ] Taken at least 30 to 60 minutes before breakfast, coffee, or other medications.
- [ ] Calcium, iron, magnesium supplements, and antacids separated by at least 4 hours.
