# Hematology Red Flag Warning Protocol

Clinical decision thresholds to distinguish routine hematology clinic discussion topics from life-threatening emergencies.

## Immediate 911 / Emergency Room Red Flags
Stop using this application and call 911 immediately if you experience:
1. **Severe Uncontrolled Bleeding**: Bleeding that will not stop after 10 minutes of firm pressure, vomiting blood, coughing up blood, or black tarry stools.
2. **Suspected Febrile Neutropenia**: Fever of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher with chills, especially within 4-6 weeks of chemotherapy or with a known low white blood cell count.
3. **Chest Pain or Shortness of Breath**: Chest discomfort, rapid heartbeat, or difficulty breathing, especially alongside a swollen leg or recent clot history (potential sign of pulmonary embolism).
4. **Signs of Stroke (FAST)**: Sudden facial drooping, unilateral arm weakness, slurred speech, or acute loss of vision.
5. **Severe Anemia Symptoms**: Fainting, chest pain, confusion, or shortness of breath at rest.
6. **Petechiae with Fever**: Widespread pinpoint red or purple spots on the skin combined with fever or feeling very unwell.
7. **Sudden Severe Headache or Vision Changes**: Especially with a known bleeding disorder or while on anticoagulation.

## Urgent Clinic Contact Signs (Call Hematologist Within 24 Hours)
Contact your hematologist's office or on-call nurse if you notice:
- New or worsening bruising without a clear cause.
- Frequent nosebleeds or gum bleeding that is becoming harder to control.
- Heavy menstrual bleeding that is new or worsening.
- Fever, chills, or feeling unwell, especially with a known low blood count.
- New fatigue, shortness of breath, or dizziness that limits daily activities.
- A new rash, especially with fever or after starting a new medication.
- Swelling, redness, or pain at an IV infusion site.
- Side effects from iron or B12 therapy that are hard to tolerate (nausea, constipation, rash, or flushing).
