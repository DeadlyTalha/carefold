# Cataract & Eye Surgery Preparation Guide

Cataract surgery is one of the most common, safe, and effective outpatient procedures performed worldwide. During surgery, the cloudy natural crystalline lens is removed using ultrasonic phacoemulsification and replaced with a clear artificial intraocular lens (IOL). This guide prepares patients for surgical consultations, lens selection decisions, and postoperative recovery.

## 1. Understanding Intraocular Lens (IOL) Options
Discussing lens implants with your cataract surgeon requires understanding trade-offs:
- **Standard Monofocal IOL**:
  - Focuses vision at a single set distance (typically set for crisp distance vision).
  - Glasses are typically required for reading, computer work, and close tasks.
  - Fully covered by standard Medicare and commercial insurance.
- **Toric Monofocal IOL**:
  - Corrects pre-existing corneal astigmatism in addition to spherical refractive error.
  - Reduces reliance on distance glasses; reading glasses still required.
  - Usually incurs an out-of-pocket refractive copay.
- **Presbyopia-Correcting IOLs (Multifocal, Extended Depth of Focus / EDOF, Accommodating)**:
  - Designed to provide clear vision across multiple focal ranges (distance, intermediate, and near).
  - May cause mild night glare, starbursts, or halos around headlights.
  - Incurs substantial out-of-pocket premium lens fees.
- **Monovision Strategy**:
  - Sets the dominant eye for distance and the non-dominant eye for near vision using monofocal lenses. Requires a trial with contact lenses first to test patient adaptation.

## 2. Pre-Operative Consultation Checklist
Bring these prioritized inquiries to your cataract evaluation:
- [ ] **Functional Impairment Discussion**: "How are my cataracts affecting my ability to drive safely at night, read fine print, or recognize faces?"
- [ ] **Corneal & Retinal Health Check**: "Are there underlying conditions (e.g., dry eye, macular degeneration, corneal dystrophy) that affect which IOL technology is safest for my eyes?"
- [ ] **Target Refraction Goal**: "Based on my daily hobbies (reading, driving, gardening, computer work), what visual outcome should we target?"
- [ ] **Second Eye Timing**: "If both eyes have cataracts, how many weeks will we wait between the first and second eye procedures?"

## 3. Post-Operative Care & Recovery Milestones
- **Protective Eye Shield**: Wear while sleeping for the first several nights to prevent accidental rubbing.
- **Prescription Eye Drop Protocol**: Strictly follow the scheduled taper of antibiotic, steroid, and NSAID drops.
- **Activity Restrictions**: Avoid heavy lifting (>10–15 lbs), bending over so the head is below the waist, and getting water directly into the eye (no swimming, hot tubs, or direct shower spray) for 1–2 weeks.

## 4. Emergency Post-Operative Red Flags
Contact your ophthalmologist's on-call line or seek emergency medical care (911) immediately if you experience:
- Sudden severe or worsening eye pain not relieved by mild over-the-counter pain medication.
- Sudden progressive decrease in vision or foggy darkening after initial improvement.
- Severe redness with increasing eyelid swelling or thick yellow/green discharge.
- New flashes of light or a dark curtain appearing in your visual field.
