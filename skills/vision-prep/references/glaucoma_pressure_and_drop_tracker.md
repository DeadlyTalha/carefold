# Glaucoma Intraocular Pressure & Drop Adherence Tracker

Glaucoma is a group of progressive optic neuropathies characterized by visual field loss, often associated with elevated intraocular pressure (IOP). Because glaucoma is largely asymptomatic in its early stages ("the sneak thief of sight"), strict daily adherence to pressure-lowering eye drops and longitudinal tracking of IOP are critical to preventing irreversible optic nerve damage.

## 1. Classes of Topical IOP-Lowering Medications
- **Prostaglandin Analogs** (e.g., Latanoprost, Travoprost, Bimatoprost, Tafluprost):
  - Dosing: Usually 1 drop once daily at bedtime.
  - Side Effects: Eye redness, darkening of iris color, eyelash lengthening, fat atrophy around eyes.
- **Beta-Blockers** (e.g., Timolol, Betaxolol):
  - Dosing: Usually 1 drop once or twice daily.
  - Precautions: Can lower heart rate and exacerbate asthma or COPD.
- **Alpha-2 Agonists** (e.g., Brimonidine):
  - Dosing: Usually 1 drop two or three times daily.
  - Side Effects: Ocular allergy, dry mouth, fatigue.
- **Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors** (e.g., Dorzolamide, Brinzolamide):
  - Dosing: Usually 1 drop two or three times daily.
- **Rho Kinase Inhibitors** (e.g., Netarsudil):
  - Dosing: Usually 1 drop once daily in the evening.

## 2. Proper Drop Administration Technique
To maximize medication delivery and minimize systemic absorption:
1. Wash hands thoroughly before instilling drops.
2. Tilt head backward, look up at the ceiling, and gently pull down the lower eyelid to form a small pocket.
3. Instill exactly 1 drop into the pocket without touching the dropper tip to the eye or skin.
4. Close eyes gently for 2 full minutes.
5. Apply gentle finger pressure to the inner corner of the eye near the nose (nasolacrimal occlusion) for 1–2 minutes.
6. If taking multiple different eye drops, wait at least 5 full minutes between medications.

## 4. Consultation Questions for Your Glaucoma Specialist
1. "Is my current measured intraocular pressure consistently meeting our target pressure goal?"
2. "How do my recent Humphrey visual field tests and OCT retinal nerve fiber layer (RNFL) scans compare to my baseline—is there any documented disease progression?"
3. "Would selective laser trabeculoplasty (SLT) or minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS) be a suitable alternative to daily eye drops?"

## 5. Acute Emergency Signs
Contact emergency medical services (911) or proceed immediately to an eye emergency department if you experience sudden severe eye pain accompanied by severe headache, nausea/vomiting, blurred vision with rainbow halos around lights, and an intensely red eye (acute closed-angle glaucoma).
