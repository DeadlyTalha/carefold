# Skin Lesion ABCDE Tracking & Discussion Guide

A clinical reference guide to the ABCDE criteria for monitoring pigmented skin lesions and preparing for professional dermoscopy evaluations.

## 1. The Clinical ABCDE Criteria

| Criterion | Definition | Warning Characteristics | Questions for Dermatologist |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A – Asymmetry** | One half of the mole does not mirror the other half | If you draw a line through the center, the two halves do not match in shape | "Does the structural asymmetry of this lesion warrant dermoscopic imaging or a biopsy?" |
| **B – Border** | The edges or margins of the lesion | Irregular, scalloped, notched, ragged, or poorly defined/blurred borders | "Are the borders concerning for dysplastic cell growth?" |
| **C – Color** | Pigment uniformity across the lesion | Multiple shades of brown or black, or patches of pink, red, white, or blue | "Is the color variegation in this mole considered stable or atypical?" |
| **D – Diameter** | Size of the lesion | Diameter larger than 6 mm (~size of a pencil eraser), or any rapidly growing lesion | "Given that this spot is over 6 mm, should we map it digitally or sample it?" |
| **E – Evolution** | Changes occurring over time (most important) | Changes in size, shape, color, elevation, or new symptoms (bleeding, itching, crusting) | "This spot has evolved over the past 3 months; what is our recommended next step?" |

## 2. Photographic Tracking Protocol
If tracking a lesion between appointments, follow standardized photographic techniques:
- **Consistent Lighting**: Photograph in bright, indirect natural daylight or consistent room illumination. Avoid harsh direct flash which creates hot spots.
- **Reference Scale**: Place a small plastic ruler or standard coin next to the mole in the photograph to provide an objective scale of measurement.
- **Close-up & Orientation**: Take two photos: one close-up in sharp focus, and one wider contextual shot showing the surrounding anatomical landmarks (e.g., forearm near elbow).
- **Date & Location Label**: Record the date and exact anatomical location immediately in your tracking log.

## 3. The "Ugly Duckling" Concept
Dermatologists often look for the "ugly duckling" sign: a mole that looks distinctively different in color, size, or pattern compared to all other moles on an individual's body. If one spot stands out as dissimilar to your body's typical pattern, point it out specifically during your examination.
