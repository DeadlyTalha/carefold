# Dermatology Body Map & Skin Check Worksheet

A comprehensive anatomical checklist for conducting monthly skin self-exams and preparing for a formal full-body dermatologist screening.

## 1. Full-Body Self-Exam Anatomical Checklist

| Anatomical Region | Front Surface Observations | Back Surface Observations | Notes / Specific Spots to Show Clinician |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Head, Face & Scalp** | Forehead, cheeks, nose, lips, ears | Back of neck, scalp (part hair with comb/dryer) | |
| **Chest & Abdomen** | Clavicles, sternum, breasts, belly | Upper back, mid back, lower back (use full mirror) | |
| **Arms & Hands** | Shoulders, biceps, forearms, palms | Triceps, back of forearms, between fingers, under nails | |
| **Groin & Buttocks** | Pelvis, thighs | Buttocks, posterior thighs | |
| **Lower Legs & Feet** | Shins, tops of feet, between toes | Calves, Achilles tendons, heels, soles of feet | |

## 2. Pre-Appointment Visit Logistics
To ensure the most thorough and efficient examination:
- **Nail Polish**: Remove all polish from fingernails and toenails so the dermatologist can examine the nail beds.
- **Makeup**: Avoid heavy foundation, blush, or powder so facial skin can be viewed under magnification.
- **Hair**: Style hair loosely (avoid tight buns or hairspray) so the scalp can be easily parted and inspected.
- **Clothing**: Wear easily removable, comfortable clothing and footwear.

## 3. Priority Questions for Your Dermatologist
- Are any of the specific spots I documented concerning for dysplasia or skin cancer?
- How frequently should I have a professional full-body skin exam based on my skin type and family history?
- What specific broad-spectrum SPF rating and sunscreen formulation do you recommend for my skin type?
- If a spot requires a biopsy, what type of biopsy (shave vs. punch) is recommended, and what is the typical healing timeline?
