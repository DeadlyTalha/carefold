# Daily Fluid and Sodium Tracking Worksheet

Use this worksheet to log fluid intake, monitor sodium intake, and recognize signs of fluid retention before your nephrology visit.

## 1. Daily Morning Weight Protocol
Weighing yourself daily under identical conditions is the most sensitive way to detect fluid retention:
- Weigh every morning immediately after your first urination.
- Weigh before eating breakfast or drinking any fluids.
- Wear identical clothing (or no clothes) and use the same calibrated scale on a hard surface.
- **Rule of Thumb**: Report a gain of >2–3 pounds in 24 hours or >5 pounds in one week to your clinic.

## 3. Dietary Sodium Awareness Checklist
- [ ] Check Nutrition Facts labels: Aim for foods with $\le 140\text{ mg}$ of sodium per serving (defined as "low sodium").
- [ ] Watch for the "Salty Six": Bread/rolls, cold cuts/cured meats, pizza, poultry injected with saline, canned soups, and restaurant sandwiches.
- [ ] Avoid salt substitutes containing potassium chloride unless explicitly approved by your nephrologist (can cause life-threatening hyperkalemia).
- [ ] Flavor foods with herbs, garlic, citrus, onion powder, and black pepper instead of table salt.

## 4. Edema Self-Check Observations
- [ ] **Pitting Ankle/Leg Edema**: Press thumb firmly into skin over shin or ankle for 5 seconds. Does an indentation remain? (Grade: 1+ mild, 2+ moderate, 3+ deep, 4+ very deep).
- [ ] **Eyelid / Facial Puffiness**: Noticeable upon waking?
- [ ] **Ring / Watch Tightness**: Difficulty removing rings from fingers compared to baseline?
- [ ] **Respiratory Symptoms**: Any shortness of breath when walking across the room or lying flat in bed? (Red flag).
