# Step-Therapy Exception Request & Appeal Workflow

Step therapy (often called "fail first" protocol) requires patients to try and fail one or more lower-cost, insurer-preferred formulary medications before the plan will authorize coverage for the drug prescribed by their physician. When a preferred drug is clinically inappropriate, ineffective, or harmful, patients and physicians can submit a Step-Therapy Exception Request.

## 1. Statutory & Clinical Grounds for Step-Therapy Exceptions
Most state step-therapy reform statutes and federal ERISA guidelines recognize five core exception criteria:
1. **Contraindication / Inadvisability**: The required step-therapy drug is clinically contraindicated or has a known adverse drug interaction with the patient's existing regimen.
2. **Prior Documented Treatment Failure**: The patient has previously tried the required formulary drug (under the current or a previous insurance plan) for an adequate duration, and the drug was ineffective in controlling symptoms.
3. **Severe Adverse Reaction**: The patient experienced a documented intolerable side effect or severe allergic reaction to the required step drug.
4. **Clinical Stability on Current Therapy**: The patient is already stabilized on the non-preferred prescribed medication and transitioning to a step drug would cause severe physical deterioration, flare-up, or withdrawal.
5. **Expected Inefficacy**: Based on clinical characteristics, genomic biomarkers, or disease staging, the required step drug is expected to be ineffective.

## 2. Assembling the Step-Therapy Appeal Dossier
Structure the appeal packet in this precise order:
- **Cover Letter**: Prescriber's formal statement requesting an immediate step-therapy override, citing the specific statutory exception criterion.
- **Trial-and-Failure Chronology Table**:

| Medication Required by Step Protocol | Trial Start Date | Trial End Date | Highest Dose Reached | Clinical Outcome / Specific Adverse Reaction | Supporting Encounter Note Date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Drug A (e.g., Methotrexate) | 2025-03-10 | 2025-06-15 | 20 mg weekly | Severe intractable nausea & elevated liver enzymes (ALT > 3x ULN) | Chart Note: 2025-06-15 |
| Drug B (e.g., Hydroxychloroquine) | 2025-07-01 | 2025-10-30 | 400 mg daily | Ineffective: Disease activity score worsened; persistent joint synovitis | Chart Note: 2025-10-30 |

- **Supporting Clinical Evidence**: Lab reports, clinical practice guidelines (e.g., American College of Rheumatology, NCCN guidelines), and FDA package insert contraindications.

## 3. Tracking Appeals & Statutory Deadlines
- Note the date and delivery confirmation receipt of the appeal submission.
- Ensure the insurer acknowledges receipt within mandated statutory timeframes (typically 24 hours for urgent cases, 72 hours for standard cases under state step-therapy laws).

## 4. Emergency Care Reminder
Do not let step therapy disputes delay urgent medical attention. Seek emergency medical care (911) immediately for acute crises, life-threatening symptoms, or severe pain.
