# ERISA & External Appeal Timeline Guide

Federal law—primarily the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 (ERISA) and the Affordable Care Act (ACA)—grants covered individuals strong procedural rights to challenge health insurance claim denials. This guide details statutory timelines, procedural requirements, and external review mechanisms.

## 1. Statutory Timeline Roadmap
Understanding statutory deadlines is essential because missing a filing window forfeits your legal right to challenge a denial.

```
Initial Denial Notice Received
         │
         ▼  (180 Days to File)
Level 1 Internal Appeal Submitted
         │
         ▼  (Insurer Must Respond Within 30–60 Days)
Level 1 Decision Issued
         │
         ├──► Overturned: Claim Processed & Paid
         └──► Upheld: Level 2 Internal Appeal (if applicable) OR
              ▼  (4 Months to File)
         External Review (Independent Review Organization / IRO)
              │
              ▼  (IRO Decision is Legally Binding on Insurer Within 45 Days)
         Final Resolution
```

### A. Level 1 Internal Appeal Filing Window: 180 Days
- You have **180 calendar days** from the date you receive the written Adverse Benefit Determination (denial notice) to file your internal appeal.
- **Payer Response Timelines**:
  - Pre-Service (Prior Authorization): Insurer must decide within **30 calendar days** (or **72 hours** for urgent/expedited care).
  - Post-Service (Retrospective Medical Claims): Insurer must decide within **60 calendar days**.

### B. Level 2 Internal Appeal (Optional / Plan-Specific)
- Some employer plans require or offer a second internal appeal level before external review. If required, the insurer typically has **30 days** to issue a decision.

### C. External Independent Medical Review: 4 Months
- If internal appeals uphold the denial on clinical grounds (medical necessity, experimental/investigational treatment, or cosmetic classification), you have the right to request an **External Review** conducted by an Independent Review Organization (IRO).
- **Filing Window**: You must file for external review within **4 months** of receiving the final internal adverse determination.
- **Binding Effect**: The decision of the independent physician reviewer is **legally binding on the insurance company**. The insurer must cover the service if the IRO overturns the denial.
- **Standard Review**: Decided within **45 calendar days**.
- **Expedited External Review**: Decided within **72 hours** if the timeline would seriously jeopardize the patient's life or health.

## 2. Right to Claim File & Review Criteria
Under ERISA Section 503 regulations (29 C.F.R. § 2560.503-1):
- You have the absolute right to request and receive, **free of charge**, reasonable access to and copies of all documents, records, and other information relevant to your claim for benefits.
- This includes the insurer's internal medical necessity criteria, medical director review notes, and credentials of the reviewing physician.

## 3. Urgent Health Warning
Claim appeal timelines apply to insurance payment disputes. In an acute medical emergency, call 911 immediately or visit the emergency department.
