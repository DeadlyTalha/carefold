# Insurance Appeal Letter Structure & Evidence Checklist

Writing an effective health insurance appeal requires clear organization, precise citation of policy language, and strong objective clinical documentation. Use this standardized template and exhibit checklist when drafting first- and second-level claim appeals.

## 1. Appeal Packet Header & Identifying Details
```markdown
Date: [YYYY-MM-DD]
To: [Insurance Appeals Department Name & Address / Secure Fax]
From: [Patient Name or Authorized Representative Name]
Member ID: [Subscriber ID #]
Group ID: [Group Number]
Claim Number: [Claim Reference #]
Date(s) of Service: [Date Range]
Attending Physician: [Physician Name & Specialty]
Service / Drug Appealed: [Name of Service, CPT/HCPCS/NDC Codes]
```

## 2. Standardized 5-Part Appeal Letter Structure

### Part 1: Formal Statement of Appeal
Explicitly state that this letter serves as a formal internal appeal under federal ERISA regulations and policy terms against the denial of claim number [Claim #] dated [Date of Denial Notice].

### Part 2: Background & Clinical Chronology
Provide a chronological narrative of the patient's condition:
- Symptoms, diagnosis date, and severity.
- Prior therapies attempted, durations, outcomes, and reasons why conservative therapies failed or are clinically inappropriate.
- The acute or chronic indication that necessitated the rendered care.

### Part 3: Rebuttal of the Insurer's Denial Reason
Directly address the specific reason stated in the Adverse Benefit Determination:
- If denied as "Not Medically Necessary": Cite definitions of Medical Necessity in the plan contract and prove how the service meets each prong.
- If denied as "Experimental / Investigational": Cite FDA approval status, peer-reviewed medical journals, and professional specialty society guidelines (e.g., NCCN, AHA, AUA).
- If denied for "Lack of Prior Authorization": Explain why pre-authorization was impossible (emergency or urgent condition under EMTALA / prudent layperson standard).

### Part 4: Physician Letter of Support & Practice Guidelines
Incorporate excerpts from the treating physician's Letter of Medical Necessity and professional practice guidelines that recommend the therapy as standard of care.

### Part 5: Specific Request & Statutory Notice
Formally request an immediate reversal of the denial and payment of the claim. Request that a full copy of the claim file and reviewer notes be provided within 30 days pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 2560.503-1.

## 3. Required Evidence Exhibits Checklist
- [ ] Exhibit A: Copy of the Original Explanation of Benefits (EOB) or Denial Notice.
- [ ] Exhibit B: Signed Letter of Medical Necessity from the Treating Physician.
- [ ] Exhibit C: Relevant Clinical Chart Notes, Operative Reports, or Discharge Summaries.
- [ ] Exhibit D: Diagnostic Test Results (Imaging, Labs, Pathology Reports).
- [ ] Exhibit E: Published Peer-Reviewed Clinical Guidelines or FDA Prescribing Information.

## 4. Emergency Care Safety Reminder
Appeal letters are administrative tools. Call 911 immediately if you experience an acute physical emergency or life-threatening condition.
