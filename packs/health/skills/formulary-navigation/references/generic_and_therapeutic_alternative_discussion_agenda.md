# Generic & Therapeutic Alternative Discussion Agenda

Prescription drug costs can often be substantially reduced by switching to bioequivalent generic equivalents or lower-tier therapeutic alternatives within the same drug class. This guide prepares patients to have informed, collaborative discussions with their doctors and pharmacists regarding affordable prescription options.

## 1. Understanding Generic vs. Therapeutic Alternatives
- **Generic Substitution (Bioequivalent)**:
  - Contains the **identical active chemical ingredient**, dosage form, safety profile, strength, route of administration, and quality as the brand-name reference drug.
  - Rated "AB" in the FDA Orange Book, meaning the generic is therapeutically equivalent and automatically interchangeable by pharmacists in most states.
  - Example: Switching from Lipitor® to generic Atorvastatin.
- **Therapeutic Interchange (Same Drug Class)**:
  - Involves switching to a **different active drug molecule** within the same therapeutic or pharmacological class that produces a similar clinical effect.
  - Requires an explicit new prescription from the physician.
  - Example: Switching from a high-tier brand PPI (e.g., Dexilant®) to a Tier 1 generic PPI (e.g., generic Omeprazole or Pantoprazole).

## 2. Physician Consultation Question Agenda
Bring these structured questions to your appointment or message your provider via the patient portal:

### A. Assessing Generic Availability
1. "Is there an FDA-approved generic equivalent available for my brand-name prescription?"
2. "If a direct generic does not exist yet, is there an older, well-established generic medication in the same drug class that would achieve the same clinical goals for me?"

### B. Therapeutic Alternatives & Safety
1. "If we switch to a generic or formulary alternative, are there differences in side effect profiles, drug interactions, or dosing frequency (e.g., once-daily vs. twice-daily)?"
2. "Will changing to an alternative require interim monitoring, such as follow-up blood pressure checks or repeat laboratory tests?"

### C. Formulation & Packaging Cost Options
1. "Does this medication come in a higher-strength tablet that can be safely split with a pill cutter (only if scored by the manufacturer)?"
2. "Can you write this prescription for a 90-day supply with three refills so I can utilize lower-cost mail order or retail maintenance copays?"

## 3. Pharmacist Consultation Checklist
Before paying for an expensive prescription at the pharmacy counter:
- [ ] "Is this prescription being processed through my health insurance, and what is the cash discount price (e.g., via GoodRx or store discount list) compared to my insurance copay?"
- [ ] "Has a manufacturer copay savings card been applied to reduce my commercial out-of-pocket cost?"
- [ ] "Is this drug subject to a prior authorization or step-therapy requirement that needs communication with my doctor's office?"

## 4. Emergency Red Flags
Cost-saving strategies must never compromise patient safety. Never discontinue, halve un-scored pills, or skip doses of critical medications without direct physician guidance. Call 911 immediately if you develop acute severe symptoms or medical crises.
