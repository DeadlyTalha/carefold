# Drug Formulary Tiers & Cost Breakdown Guide

A drug formulary is a continually updated list of prescription medications approved for coverage by a health insurance plan or Pharmacy Benefit Manager (PBM). Formularies are typically divided into tiers, which determine the patient's out-of-pocket cost-sharing (flat copay or percentage coinsurance).

## 1. Standard 5-Tier Formulary Structure

| Tier Level | Designation | Typical Medications | Cost-Sharing Model | Patient Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1** | Preferred Generic | High-utilization, proven bioequivalent generic drugs (e.g., Lisinopril, Metformin, Atorvastatin) | Lowest flat copay (typically \$0–\$10) | Default first-line choice for maximum savings |
| **Tier 2** | Non-Preferred Generic / Preferred Brand | Higher-cost generics or established preferred brand drugs with no generic equivalent | Moderate flat copay (typically \$15–\$45) | Explore whether an alternative Tier 1 generic exists |
| **Tier 3** | Preferred Brand | Brand-name drugs on the plan's preferred list with clinical advantages or manufacturer rebate agreements | Higher flat copay (typically \$50–\$100) | Check for manufacturer copay cards |
| **Tier 4** | Non-Preferred Brand | Brand-name medications not on the preferred list, often with lower-cost formulary alternatives available | High coinsurance (typically 30%–50%) | Request formulary exception or switch to preferred brand |
| **Tier 5** | Specialty | High-cost biologic, infused, or oral targeted oncology/autoimmune drugs (typically >\$830/month) | Percentage coinsurance (typically 25%–33%) | Access Patient Assistance Programs (PAPs) or co-pay foundations |

## 2. Formulary Utilization Management Rules
Insurer restrictions that impact access beyond tier placement:
- **Prior Authorization (PA)**: Requires prescriber to submit clinical justification before the pharmacy can dispense.
- **Step Therapy (ST)**: Requires trial and failure of Tier 1 or Tier 2 alternatives before approving Tier 3 or Tier 4 drugs.
- **Quantity Limits (QL)**: Restricts the maximum number of doses dispensed per 30- or 90-day window (e.g., max 30 tablets per 30 days, or max 4 migraine abortive doses per month).

## 3. Retail Pharmacy vs. 90-Day Mail Order Savings
Many health plans offer significant cost savings for maintenance medications used for chronic conditions (e.g., hypertension, diabetes, cholesterol):
- **30-Day Retail Supply**: Patient pays 3 individual monthly copays (e.g., \$15 x 3 = \$45 for 90 days).
- **90-Day Mail Order Supply**: Plan frequently charges only 2 or 2.5 copays for a full 90-day shipment (e.g., \$30 total for 90 days), with free doorstep delivery.

## 4. Emergency Care Reminder
Formulary navigation is an administrative cost-reduction guide. Never stop taking life-sustaining medications (such as insulin, antiarrhythmics, or blood thinners) due to cost without consulting your physician. Call 911 immediately if you experience an acute physical emergency or life-threatening symptoms.
