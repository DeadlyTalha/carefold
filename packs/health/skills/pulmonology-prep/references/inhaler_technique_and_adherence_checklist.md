# Inhaler Technique Verification & Adherence Checklist

Studies show that up to 70% of patients do not use their inhalers with optimal technique. Use this checklist to review your device technique with your pulmonologist or respiratory therapist.

## 1. Metered-Dose Inhalers (MDI - Press and Breathe)
- [ ] **Cap Removal & Shake**: Shake the inhaler well for 5 seconds before every actuation.
- [ ] **Spacer / Valved Holding Chamber**: Using a spacer significantly increases drug delivery to the lungs and reduces throat deposition.
- [ ] **Exhale Fully**: Breathe out gently away from the inhaler to empty the lungs.
- [ ] **Slow, Deep Inhalation**: Press down on the canister while breathing in slowly and deeply over 3–5 seconds.
- [ ] **Breath-Hold**: Hold breath for 10 seconds (or as long as comfortable) to allow aerosol particles to settle.
- [ ] **Rinse & Spit**: If the inhaler contains a corticosteroid, rinse mouth thoroughly with water and spit it out to prevent oral thrush and hoarseness.

## 2. Dry Powder Inhalers (DPI - e.g. Diskus, Turbuhaler, Ellipta)
- [ ] **Keep Horizontal**: Hold device level while loading the dose.
- [ ] **Never Exhale Into Device**: Moisture from breath can clump the dry medication powder.
- [ ] **Quick, Deep Inhalation**: Unlike MDIs, DPIs require a fast, forceful, deep breath to disperse the powder.
- [ ] **Breath-Hold**: Hold breath for up to 10 seconds.

## 3. Adherence Tracking Worksheet
Record the questions below to review with your provider:
- How many days in the past 30 days did you miss your controller inhaler dose?
- Are you experiencing barriers such as high copays, difficulty depressing the canister, or unpleasant taste?
- Does your rescue inhaler have an integrated dose counter to avoid running out unexpectedly?
