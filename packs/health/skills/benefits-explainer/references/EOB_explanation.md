# Explanation of Benefits (EOB) Guide

A comprehensive, plain-language reference for understanding and reconciling health insurance Explanation of Benefits (EOB) statements.

---

## 1. What is an Explanation of Benefits (EOB)?

An **Explanation of Benefits (EOB)** is an informational statement sent by your health insurance company after a healthcare provider submits a claim for services you received.

> **CRITICAL RULE**: An EOB is **NOT A BILL**. Do not send payment to your insurance company or provider based on an EOB alone.

The EOB informs you:
- Which medical services, procedures, or medications were submitted by your healthcare provider.
- Whether the services were approved, discounted, or denied by your insurance plan.
- How much your insurance plan paid directly to your doctor, facility, or pharmacy.
- The maximum out-of-pocket amount you may legitimately owe the provider once they bill you.

---

## 2. Key Components of an EOB Statement

Every EOB contains several standardized sections. When reviewing your statement, check each item carefully:

### A. Identification & Claim Details
- **Patient Name & Member ID**: Confirms which family member received care under the policy.
- **Claim Number**: A unique tracking number assigned by your insurer. Always have this number ready when calling customer service or the billing office.
- **Healthcare Provider / Facility**: The name of the physician, therapist, laboratory, or hospital that rendered the service.
- **Date of Service (DOS)**: The exact calendar date on which the visit, diagnostic test, or procedure occurred.
- **Service Description / Billing Codes**: CPT (Current Procedural Terminology) codes, HCPCS codes, or plain-English descriptions (e.g., `99214` - Established Office Visit, `70553` - Brain MRI).

### B. Financial Breakdown & Cost-Sharing Columns

| Column Name | What It Means |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Billed Amount (Charged)** | The full list price charged by the provider for the service before any insurance discounts. |
| **Allowed Amount (Contracted Rate)** | The maximum fee your insurance plan agrees is reasonable for that service under their in-network provider contract. |
| **Plan Discount (Contractual Adjustment)** | The dollar difference between the Billed Amount and the Allowed Amount. An in-network provider agrees by contract to write off this amount; you **cannot** be billed for this difference. |
| **Deductible Applied** | The portion of the allowed charge you must pay because your annual deductible has not yet been met. |
| **Copay Amount** | A flat predetermined dollar fee (e.g., \$25 or \$50) required by your plan for that specific type of visit. |
| **Coinsurance Amount** | Your percentage share (e.g., 20%) of the allowed amount after the deductible has been satisfied. |
| **Amount Paid by Plan** | The actual dollar amount your health insurance carrier paid directly to the provider. |
| **Patient Responsibility (You Owe)** | The final cumulative amount (Deductible + Copay + Coinsurance + any non-covered charges) that the provider is authorized to collect from you. |

## 3. Understanding Common Claim Remark & Denial Codes

If an item on your EOB was not paid at 100%, the line item will show a remark code or footnote explaining why:

1. **Contractual Obligation (CO)**: In-network provider fee adjustments that are written off and cannot be collected from the patient.
2. **Prior Authorization Required**: The procedure or medication required pre-service authorization that was not obtained or is under clinical dispute.
3. **Coordination of Benefits (COB)**: The insurer needs updated information to determine whether you have secondary health coverage before paying the claim.
4. **Non-Covered Service**: The specific test or therapy is excluded under the employer's plan policy documents.
5. **Medical Records Requested**: The payer requested additional clinical notes from the doctor before adjudicating the claim.

## 4. Step-by-Step EOB Reconciliation Process

When receiving healthcare services, follow this four-step reconciliation workflow:

1. **Retain the EOB**: Save the physical EOB or download the PDF from your insurance portal into a dedicated folder or Carefold attachments directory.
2. **Hold Payment Until Provider Invoicing**: Never pay estimated bills at check-in or prior to claim adjudication unless it is a standard office visit copay. Wait until the doctor's office sends their formal invoice.
3. **Match Line Items**: Compare the provider's bill to the insurer's EOB line by line:
   - Does the Date of Service match?
   - Does the Provider Name match?
   - Does the **Patient Balance** on the provider's bill match the **Patient Responsibility** line on your EOB?
4. **Resolve Discrepancies**:
   - If the provider bills more than the EOB allowed patient responsibility: Call provider billing and provide the EOB Claim Number. Remind them of their in-network contractual discount.
   - If the claim was denied for missing information or lack of prior auth: Contact the ordering physician's billing coordinator to file a corrected claim or appeal.

---

## 5. Protections Under the No Surprises Act

If you received emergency care, or received non-emergency care from an out-of-network provider at an in-network facility without giving prior written consent:
- Federal law (**The No Surprises Act**) protects you from "balance billing".
- You can only be billed at your plan's standard in-network cost-sharing rate (copay/coinsurance).
- Any balance billing from out-of-network clinicians above the in-network cost-sharing is prohibited.
