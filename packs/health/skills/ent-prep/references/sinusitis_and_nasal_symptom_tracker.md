# Chronic Sinusitis & Nasal Symptom Tracker

Chronic rhinosinusitis (CRS) is characterized by prolonged mucosal inflammation of the nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses lasting 12 weeks or longer. This tracker helps patients record the cardinal symptoms of sinus disease—nasal obstruction, facial pain/pressure, anterior or posterior discharge, and decreased sense of smell (hyposmia)—to present clear longitudinal data to an otolaryngologist.

## 1. Cardinal Sinus Symptom Scoring
Rate symptoms daily on a 0–3 severity scale:
- **0**: None / Absent
- **1**: Mild (Present, easily tolerated)
- **2**: Moderate (Bothersome, interferes with concentration or sleep)
- **3**: Severe (Significant daily distress, incapacitating)

## 2. Anatomical Location of Facial Pressure
Check all regions where fullness or dull aching pain is experienced:
- **Maxillary**: Cheeks, upper teeth, jaw.
- **Frontal**: Forehead, above eyebrows (often worse when leaning forward).
- **Ethmoidal**: Between the eyes, bridge of nose.
- **Sphenoidal**: Deep vertex of head, occipital pain behind eyes.

## 3. Consultation Discussion Agenda for Your ENT
1. "Given my 12+ weeks of facial pressure and congestion, does my clinical presentation meet the criteria for chronic rhinosinusitis with or without nasal polyps?"
2. "Would a dedicated non-contrast sinus CT scan or in-office nasal endoscopy be helpful to assess the patency of my osteomeatal complexes?"
3. "Am I using optimal technique for my high-volume saline nasal irrigations and topical intranasal corticosteroid sprays?"
4. "If maximal medical therapy fails to resolve my symptoms, am I a candidate for functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS) or balloon sinuplasty?"

## 4. Urgent Warning Signs (Emergency Evaluation)
Sinus infections rarely spread to orbital or intracranial structures, but warning signs require immediate emergency care (911 or emergency department):
- Swelling, redness, or protrusion of the eyeball (proptosis).
- Double vision (diplopia) or decreased visual acuity.
- Severe forehead swelling or doughy mass (Pott's puffy tumor).
- Severe stiff neck accompanied by high fever, vomiting, and mental confusion.
