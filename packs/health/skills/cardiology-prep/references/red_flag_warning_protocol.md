# Cardiovascular Red Flag Warning Protocol

Clinical decision thresholds to distinguish routine clinic visit discussion topics from life-threatening emergencies.

## Immediate 911 / Emergency Room Red Flags
Stop using this application and call 911 immediately if you experience:
1. **Acute Chest Discomfort**: Crushing, squeezing, heavy pressure, or burning in the chest, particularly if lasting more than 5 minutes or worsening.
2. **Radiation of Discomfort**: Pain spreading to the jaw, neck, throat, back, left shoulder, or down either arm.
3. **Associated Symptoms**: Shortness of breath, diaphoresis (sudden profuse cold sweat), unexplained nausea, lightheadedness, or vomiting.
4. **Syncope**: Sudden unexplained loss of consciousness or fainting, particularly during physical exertion.
5. **Severe Acute Dyspnea**: Inability to breathe while resting or inability to speak more than a couple of words without pausing.
6. **Stroke Signs (FAST)**: Sudden facial drooping, unilateral arm weakness, slurred speech, or acute loss of vision.

## Urgent Clinic Contact Signs (Call Physician Within 24 Hours)
Contact your cardiologist's office or on-call nurse if you notice:
- Rapid unexplained weight gain of 3 or more pounds in a single day, or 5 pounds in a week (potential sign of fluid retention in heart failure).
- Noticeable increase in ankle, lower leg, or abdominal swelling.
- Needing extra pillows to sleep at night to prevent waking up short of breath (orthopnea).
- Persistent resting blood pressure readings above 180/120 mmHg without chest pain or acute symptoms (hypertensive urgency).
- Increasing frequency of palpitations that resolve on their own without dizziness or fainting.
