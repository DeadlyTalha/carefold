# Acute Red Flags Directory Across Organ Systems

## 1. Cardiovascular Emergencies
- Crushing retrosternal chest pain, heaviness, or squeezing pressure.
- Pain radiating to the jaw, neck, back, epigastrium, or left arm.
- Sudden unexplained syncope (fainting), near-syncope, or persistent ventricular arrhythmia symptoms.
- Acute diaphoresis accompanied by pallor and nausea during chest tightness.

## 2. Respiratory Emergencies
- Severe acute dyspnea (inability to speak full sentences).
- Stridor, upper airway obstruction, or wheezing with cyanosis (blue lips or nail beds).
- Massive hemoptysis (coughing up fresh blood).
- Sudden pleuritic chest pain with rapid respiratory decompensation (suspected pulmonary embolism or pneumothorax).

## 3. Neurological Emergencies
- Sudden facial drooping or asymmetrical smile.
- Sudden unilateral weakness or numbness in an arm or leg.
- Sudden speech difficulty (aphasia, dysarthria, slurred words).
- Sudden severe "thunderclap" headache (worst headache of life).
- Sudden loss of vision, diplopia, or acute ataxia.
- Active or prolonged seizure activity (> 5 minutes or status epilepticus).

## 4. Systemic, Allergic & Surgical Emergencies
- Anaphylaxis: Acute facial, tongue, or pharyngeal swelling with respiratory difficulty or hypotension.
- Acute abdomen: Rigid board-like abdomen, severe rebound tenderness, or signs of peritoneal sepsis.
- Uncontrolled external hemorrhaging or signs of hypovolemic shock (cold clammy skin, confusion, profound tachycardia).
- Acute suicidal ideation, intent, or self-harm emergency.
