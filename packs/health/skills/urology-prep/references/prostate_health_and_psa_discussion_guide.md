# Prostate Health & PSA Discussion Guide

Prostate-specific antigen (PSA) is a glycoprotein enzyme produced by the prostate gland. While PSA testing is widely used as a screening and monitoring tool, elevated levels can result from benign conditions as well as malignancies. This guide prepares patients to have informed discussions with their urologist regarding PSA results and prostate health.

## 1. Understanding PSA Test Nuances
- **Total PSA**: The total concentration of PSA in the blood (typically reported in ng/mL). General reference ranges vary by age, but values above 4.0 ng/mL often prompt further clinical evaluation.
- **Free PSA & Percent Free PSA (%fPSA)**: Unbound PSA circulating in the blood. A lower percentage of free PSA (<10–15%) may indicate higher risk, whereas higher %fPSA (>25%) suggests benign causes.
- **PSA Velocity & Doubling Time**: The rate of change in PSA over consecutive tests. A rapid rise is often more clinically significant than an isolated stable elevation.
- **Benign Factors Elevating PSA**:
  - Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH) / prostate enlargement.
  - Prostatitis / urinary tract infection.
  - Recent ejaculation (within 48 hours).
  - Recent vigorous bicycling or strenuous physical activity.
  - Digital rectal examination (DRE) or urinary catheterization.

## 2. Structured Consultation Questions

### A. Contextualizing Lab Findings
1. "Given my age, symptoms, and previous test history, does my PSA elevation warrant immediate repeat testing or a secondary biomarker test (e.g., Free PSA, 4Kscore, Prostate Health Index / PHI)?"
2. "Could my current urinary symptoms (weak stream, nighttime waking) be explained primarily by benign prostate enlargement?"

### B. Advanced Diagnostic Options
1. "Would a multiparametric MRI (mpMRI) of the prostate be appropriate before considering a biopsy?"
2. "If an MRI is performed, what is the PI-RADS score and what does it indicate about the need for a targeted biopsy?"
3. "If a biopsy is recommended, is it performed via the transrectal (TRUS) or transperineal approach, and what are the respective infection risks?"

### C. Management Strategies for Benign Enlargement (BPH)
1. "What are the benefits and potential sexual/cardiovascular side effects of medical therapies (such as alpha-blockers like tamsulosin or 5-ARIs like finasteride)?"
2. "Are there minimally invasive procedural options (e.g., UroLift, Rezum water vapor therapy, HoLEP, TURP) suitable for my prostate anatomy?"

## 3. Emergency Red Flags
Consultations must never replace emergency medical care. Seek emergency medical evaluation (911) immediately if you experience acute total urinary retention (inability to pass urine with painful bladder distension), visible blood in urine with passage of obstructive clots, or high fever with shaking chills and difficulty urinating.
