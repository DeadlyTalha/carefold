# Urology Appointment Checklist & Procedure Preparation Guide

This comprehensive checklist helps patients prepare for initial consultations, routine follow-ups, and common in-office procedures with a urologist.

## 1. Pre-Appointment Preparation Checklist
- [ ] **Complete Urinalysis / Urine Culture**: Check if a clean-catch urine specimen is required upon arrival at the clinic. Avoid voiding immediately before checking in unless instructed otherwise.
- [ ] **Compile Medication List**: Include all prescription drugs, over-the-counter pain relievers, cold medications (decongestants can worsen urinary retention), and dietary supplements.
- [ ] **3-Day Voiding Diary**: Bring completed frequency-volume charts showing daytime voids, nocturia frequency, and fluid intake.
- [ ] **Prior Diagnostic Reports**: Bring printed copies or upload attachments of previous PSA tests, renal/bladder ultrasound reports, CT scans of the abdomen/pelvis, and previous operative notes.

## 2. Preparing for Common In-Office Urological Procedures

### A. Flexible Cystoscopy
- **Description**: An examination of the urethra and bladder using a thin, flexible lighted camera.
- **Preparation**: Typically requires no fasting. Local anesthetic gel (lidocaine) is applied to the urethra.
- **Questions to Ask**:
  1. "Will I experience mild burning or pink-tinged urine for the first 24–48 hours following the procedure?"
  2. "Should I take a single prophylactic antibiotic dose prior to or following the cystoscopy?"

### B. Urodynamic Studies (UDS)
- **Description**: Evaluates bladder filling pressure, storage capacity, and flow rate during voiding.
- **Preparation**: Arrive with a comfortably full bladder for the initial uroflowmetry test.
- **Questions to Ask**:
  1. "Should I discontinue my bladder medications (e.g., mirabegron, oxybutynin) a few days prior to the test?"
  2. "How will the results guide decisions between medication and surgical intervention?"

### C. Renal / Bladder Ultrasound & Post-Void Residual (PVR)
- **Preparation**: Involves scanning the bladder when full, voiding completely, and scanning again to measure residual urine volume.
- **Normal Benchmark**: Post-void residual volume <50–100 mL is generally considered normal emptying in healthy adults.

## 3. Post-Visit Action Plan & Tracking
- **Document Follow-up Schedule**: Note when repeat PSA testing, ultrasound imaging, or lab surveillance is scheduled.
- **Medication Instructions**: Confirm timing and expected onset of symptom relief for any prescribed therapies.

## 4. Urgent Red Flag Warnings
Do not wait for a clinic appointment. Call 911 or visit the emergency room immediately if you experience acute total urinary retention, gross hematuria with heavy clots, high fever with severe flank pain and chills, or acute sudden testicular pain.
