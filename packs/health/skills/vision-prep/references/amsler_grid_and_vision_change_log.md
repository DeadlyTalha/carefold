# Amsler Grid Self-Monitoring Protocol & Vision Change Log

The Amsler grid is an essential home screening tool used to detect early signs of retinal distortion (metamorphopsia) or central blind spots (scotomas) caused by age-related macular degeneration (AMD), diabetic macular edema, or epiretinal membranes.

## 1. Testing Instructions
Perform this self-check in a well-lit room wearing your normal reading glasses:
1. **Distance**: Hold or position the grid approximately 12 to 14 inches (30–35 cm) from your face.
2. **Monocular Testing**: Cover your left eye completely with your hand. Test your right eye first.
3. **Central Fixation**: Look directly at the center black dot on the grid.
4. **Observation**: While maintaining fixation on the central dot, observe the surrounding lines and squares using your peripheral vision. Ask yourself:
   - Do all the lines look completely straight and parallel, or do any appear wavy, curved, or distorted?
   - Are any squares missing, blurred, gray, or blacked out?
   - Do all four corners and sides of the grid appear intact?
5. **Repeat**: Cover your right eye and repeat the entire procedure for your left eye.

## 3. Consultation Question Framework for Retina Specialists
1. "Given my dry macular degeneration baseline, what specific changes on my Amsler grid indicate an urgent transition to wet AMD requiring anti-VEGF injections?"
2. "Does my optical coherence tomography (OCT) scan show any subretinal or intraretinal fluid accumulation?"
3. "Are AREDS-2 antioxidant/zinc supplements appropriate for my current stage of macular health?"

## 4. Immediate Ophthalmic Emergencies
Do not wait for a routine checkup. Seek emergency eye care (call 911 or visit an eye emergency trauma center) immediately if you experience:
- Sudden dark curtain or veil pulling across your field of vision (potential retinal detachment).
- Sudden shower of dark floating specks accompanied by bright flashes of light.
- Sudden severe painless loss of sight in one or both eyes.
- Sudden excruciating red eye pain accompanied by colored halos around lights, headache, and vomiting (acute angle-closure glaucoma).
