# Clinical Trial Discussion Checklist & Patient Evaluation Guide

Clinical trials evaluate new cancer drugs, novel combinations, and improved therapeutic techniques. When exploring clinical trials, patients and caregivers need structured questions to evaluate eligibility, risks, potential benefits, and logistical expectations.

## 1. Understanding Clinical Trial Phases
- **Phase I**: Evaluates safety, safe dosage ranges, and identifies side effects in a small group of participants (usually 15–30 patients).
- **Phase II**: Evaluates whether the investigational drug or combination has an active anticancer effect in a specific tumor type (usually 100–300 patients).
- **Phase III**: Compares the investigational treatment against the current standard-of-care in a large cohort (hundreds or thousands of patients). May involve randomization.
- **Phase IV**: Post-marketing surveillance to gather additional information on long-term safety and optimal use.

## 2. Eligibility & Screening Inquiries
Use this checklist when discussing potential trials with your oncology care team:
- [ ] **Eligibility Criteria**: "Do my exact cancer stage, histological subtype, prior lines of treatment, and molecular biomarkers meet the inclusion criteria?"
- [ ] **Exclusion Factors**: "Are there specific exclusion criteria (e.g., previous immunotherapies, organ function thresholds, autoimmune history) that disqualify me?"
- [ ] **Screening Window**: "What additional baseline tests (new biopsy, fresh imaging, specialized blood panels) are required during the screening phase?"

## 3. Study Design & Randomization Questions
- [ ] **Treatment Arms**: "Is this an open-label single-arm study, or is it randomized?"
- [ ] **Control Group**: "If the trial is randomized, what does the control arm receive? (In cancer trials, control arms typically receive current standard-of-care, not an empty placebo)."
- [ ] **Crossover Provision**: "If the disease progresses while on the control arm, is there a crossover provision that allows me to receive the investigational drug?"

## 4. Logistical, Schedule & Financial Considerations
- [ ] **Visit Frequency**: "How often will I need to travel to the trial medical center for infusions, pharmacokinetic blood draws, and monitoring visits?"
- [ ] **Inpatient Stays**: "Are overnight stays required for observation during early infusions?"
- [ ] **Financial Coverage**: "Who pays for the experimental drug, specialized research scans, and extra clinic visits? (The trial sponsor usually covers research costs, while standard insurance covers routine care)."
- [ ] **Travel Assistance**: "Are there patient travel grants or lodging stipends provided by the study sponsor?"

## 5. Decision-Making & Patient Rights
- **Voluntary Participation**: Participation in a clinical trial is always voluntary.
- **Informed Consent**: The patient can withdraw consent and leave the study at any time without compromising ongoing standard oncology care.
- **Emergency Safety**: Always report any sudden acute illness or severe symptom flare to the trial investigator hotline and seek emergency medical care (911) for life-threatening crises.
