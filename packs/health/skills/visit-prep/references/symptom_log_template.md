## 3. Symptom Characteristics & Clinical Descriptors

When describing your symptoms to your clinician, consider these specific dimensions:
- **Quality & Sensation**: Is it sharp, dull, throbbing, burning, aching, shooting, tingling, or crampy?
- **Location & Radiation**: Where does it start? Does it spread anywhere else (e.g., radiating down the arm or leg)?
- **Timing & Rhythm**: Is it constant, intermittent, waxing and waning, or tied to specific times of day (morning stiffness vs. evening fatigue)?
- **Aggravating Factors**: What activities, foods, postures, or stressors reliably make it worse?
- **Alleviating Factors**: What positions, rest periods, temperature applications (heat/ice), or home remedies provide relief?

## 6. Questions to Ask Your Clinician

Prioritize these questions during your visit:
1. *Based on this timeline, what are the most likely causes of these symptoms?*
2. *Are there any diagnostic tests or labs that would help clarify what is going on?*
3. *What non-medication lifestyle adjustments, physical therapy, or supportive measures do you recommend?*
4. *What specific warning signs or "red flags" should prompt me to contact your office or seek urgent care?*
5. *When should we schedule a follow-up to re-evaluate if symptoms do not improve?*

---

## ⚠️ Important Boundary Disclosures

> **NON-CLINICAL INFORMATIONAL TEMPLATE**: This log is an administrative organizational aid designed to help you communicate clearly with your doctor or licensed healthcare provider. It does **NOT** provide clinical diagnoses, medical interpretations, or treatment recommendations.
> 
> **EMERGENCY WARNING**: If you experience severe acute symptoms—such as crushing chest pain, sudden numbness or weakness on one side of the face/body, difficulty speaking, severe breathing distress, sudden severe headache, or severe trauma—do not wait for an appointment. **Call 911 or visit the nearest emergency department immediately.**
